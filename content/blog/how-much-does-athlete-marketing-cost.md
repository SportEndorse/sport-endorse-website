Ask an agency what athlete marketing costs and you'll get "it depends" followed by an invoice. This post is the version we'd want as a buyer: the actual numbers, what drives them, and where budgets go wrong.

## The three cost layers

Athlete marketing spend breaks into three parts, and confusing them is where most budgeting mistakes start.

**1. Access.** How you find and contract athletes. Through a traditional agency this cost is opaque — retainers plus mark-ups baked into athlete fees. On a platform it's explicit: Sport Endorse brand subscriptions are market-based, from €999/£999 per year (or $3,000 for US brands), rising to €1,800/£1,200/$6,000 per year for your domestic athlete market, with quarterly options from €360. Each athlete market is subscribed separately, so a brand campaigning only in Ireland pays for Europe, not the world.

**2. Deal commission.** Marketplaces take a cut of each deal. The US norm runs up to 30%. Sport Endorse charges a transparent 14–18% depending on deal value, agreed openly at deal time — and unlike agency mark-ups, everyone can see it.

**3. Athlete fees.** The fee the athlete actually receives, agreed deal by deal. This is the layer with real variance, and it's driven by five factors: audience size, engagement rate, sport and market, deliverable scope (a single post versus a season-long ambassadorship), and usage rights — the more content reuse and exclusivity you want, the more you pay.

## What that means in practice

A single product-seeding collaboration with a verified athlete can start in the hundreds of euros. A national ambassador programme with appearance days, content rights and exclusivity is a five-figure commitment. The difference isn't a mystery — it's scope, and a good brief controls it.

## Where budgets go wrong

Three patterns we see repeatedly: paying for follower counts instead of engagement (a 70K-follower athlete at 6% engagement out-delivers a 300K account at 1%); leaving usage rights undefined until after content performs, when renegotiating is expensive; and treating athlete marketing as a one-off test rather than an always-on programme — annual subscribers save roughly a third versus quarterly billing for exactly this reason.

## The honest summary

For a European brand: from about €83 a month for cross-border platform access, €150 a month for your domestic market on an annual plan, a 14–18% commission on deals, plus athlete fees you agree transparently per deal. Compare that with what a single agency-brokered campaign costs — and, more importantly, with knowing exactly where every euro went.
